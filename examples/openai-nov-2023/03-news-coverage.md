# Industry reels as OpenAI fires Sam Altman

> Demo content — paraphrased third-party coverage of the events of 17 November 2023. Not original journalism; not from any specific outlet.

OpenAI, the artificial intelligence company best known for ChatGPT, removed chief executive Sam Altman on Friday, in a decision the company's board attributed to a loss of confidence following an internal review.

The announcement caught most of the AI industry by surprise. Altman had been a defining public face of OpenAI since its founding and had presided over a period of unprecedented growth following the launch of ChatGPT in November 2022.

Mira Murati, the company's chief technology officer, has been named interim chief executive. Murati joined OpenAI in 2018 and has led much of the company's product development, including the deployment of ChatGPT and the DALL·E image generation system.

Co-founder Greg Brockman has also been removed from his role as chairman of the board, though OpenAI said he would remain at the company as president. The remaining board members include OpenAI co-founder and chief scientist Ilya Sutskever, Quora chief executive Adam D'Angelo, technology executive Tasha McCauley, and Georgetown Center for Security and Emerging Technology director Helen Toner.

The decision has prompted immediate questions about OpenAI's relationship with Microsoft, which invested more than ten billion dollars in the company earlier this year and integrates OpenAI's models into its Azure and Copilot products. A Microsoft spokesperson said the company remained committed to its partnership with OpenAI.

It is unclear what role, if any, Altman will take next. People familiar with the matter described the decision as having been made quickly and without consultation with major investors.
